## Reflection

1. **The three most frustrating or time-consuming parts:**

   Finding reliable economic data, determining which information was relevant to the business, and understanding how economic changes could affect operating expenses.

2. **What would make me trust a system's recommendation? What would make me ignore it?**

   I would trust the assistant if it provided accurate information from reliable sources and explained how it reached its conclusions. I would question recommendations that lacked supporting evidence.

3. **What a system should never do on my behalf without asking:**

   The assistant should ask permission before changing financial records, adjusting budgets, sharing sensitive business information, or making financial decisions.

4. **How I would know it was actually helping** (this becomes my value hypothesis):

   The assistant would be useful if it reduced the time needed to collect and analyze economic information while helping me make informed decisions. I could measure this by comparing the time spent and the accuracy of decisions before and after using the assistant.

5. **One assumption about my own workflow that this log proved wrong:**

   I initially thought reviewing economic conditions would be straightforward, but I realized that finding information is only part of the process. Understanding how that information relates to a business's actual expenses is more challenging.
